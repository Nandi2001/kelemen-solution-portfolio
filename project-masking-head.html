All projects/Automated masking head

Bachelor’s thesis · Mechanical design · 2024

Automated
masking head.

A masking and extraction head for a Cartesian wall-painting robot. Retractable blades protect adjacent walls, while a floating enclosure lets the spray gun reach the corners. Designed to reduce manual masking and contain paint overspray.

The design thinking

Three ideas behind the head.

Mask only where it matters

Lead-screw actuators move the masking blades along dovetail guides. Angled edges and silicone wipers limit contact with the neighbouring wall.

Let the gun reach the corner

Two perpendicular linear guides allow the enclosure to move relative to the gun. Springs return it to centre after the corner pass.

Design for cleaning and repair

Extraction ports connect the enclosure to a vacuum system. Replaceable guides and a detachable box subassembly make servicing part of the design.

From assembly to component

A look inside the drawing set.

Cropped samples from my 2024 bachelor’s thesis drawing annex, Technical University of Cluj-Napoca. Created with SOLIDWORKS Educational Product, for instructional use.

Manufacturing & development

Designed with the next step in mind.

The thesis includes actuator and spring sizing, material selection, static simulations and manufacturing routes for the mounting plate and rail support: waterjet cutting, milling, drilling and threading.

This is a mechanical design study awaiting physical testing. Further development would focus on extraction airflow, automated head rotation and extending coverage around windows, doors and ceilings.
